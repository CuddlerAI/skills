# Director template — character clips

The house standard for a character talking to the viewer, rewritten for agents. It reflects what held up across several in-house test batches: a hand on the lens, a long head turn, a knock at the door or a stray music bed each ruined takes, and the rules below are the fixes that worked.

## 1. 15-second one-take: five beats

| Beat | Rough time | Must have | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① Open + plant | 0–3 s | The scene is already under way; one natural action puts the prop in frame; eye contact; **line 1** | Line 1 ≤ 8 words and acknowledges that the viewer is here ("You made it.") |
| ② Blocking | 3–6 s | The staging's one camera or blocking move | Eye level, hands busy, never a full-face close-up |
| ③ Gesture + line 2 | 6–9 s | One or two small gestures; **line 2** | Teases or sizes up the viewer |
| ④ Payoff | about 8–11 s | The prop makes one object sound; eye-only glance; a flicker of reaction | Don't describe the sound again afterwards |
| ⑤ Hook + hold | 10–15 s | **Line 3 = the hook**; finished by second 13; lips close; one held expression; two seconds of eye contact | ≤ 14 words: a choice, a dare or a secret |

Write only two times into the prompt: "the first line starts before second 2" and "she finishes this line by second 13". Everything else is order, not seconds.

## 2. Five stagings (rotate them; two takes never share one)

1. **Mid-task** — she is doing a small task that fits the place (folding a scarf, polishing a glass, leafing through a notebook) and looks up to speak while still doing it. Camera at eye level, medium shot, nearly still; on line 2 she puts the thing down and leans in a little.
2. **Walk-in** — she starts deep in the frame, facing the camera, and takes two or three steps toward it as she speaks. Camera at eye level, still; she stops in a chest-up medium close shot, no closer.
3. **Profile turn** — she stands side-on with her profile clear; as the first line leaves her lips she turns to face the camera. Then a slow eye-level push-in to waist-up, and stop.
4. **Seated** — on a chair, sofa or bar stool with the prop in hand, she looks up and speaks. Camera at her seated eye line, medium shot, nearly still; she stays seated and leans in on line 2.
5. **Leaning** — against a counter, railing, car or windowsill, one hand on the surface, the other holding the prop. Camera at eye level, slow push from medium to chest-up, then stop.

## 3. Prop, payoff, sound

| Plant (beat ①) | Payoff (beat ④) | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Phone face-down on the table | It buzzes and its edge lights up | Buzz against the tabletop |
| Kettle on a stove | It starts to whistle | Rising whistle |
| A glass of ice | The ice cracks | Crisp crack |
| Keys on a counter | They slide off and clatter | Metallic clatter |
| Rain clouds in the window | Rain starts hitting the glass | Tapping rain, low thunder |
| A record playing | The needle skips | Vinyl skip |
| A street or parking lot behind her | A horn, or an engine starting | Horn or engine |
| A station or airport bench | An announcement echoes | Chime and echo |
| A cat asleep on the sofa | It knocks something off a shelf | Soft thud |

Pick the one that fits the place and sets up the hook. Never use doorbells, knocks, footsteps or anyone arriving — they put a second person in the frame.

## 4. Small gestures (pick one or two)

Brushes hair out of her eyes · tilts her head · tucks hair behind her ear · smiles with narrowed eyes · takes a slow breath · raises an eyebrow · glances down and back up.

## 5. Held expression at the end (rotate)

A smile · a raised eyebrow · a pressed-lips smile · smiling eyes · a slight head tilt.

## 6. Closing line and safety tail

Closing line (fill the brackets):

> The whole clip keeps a playful, warm tension; one continuous long take with no cuts and no transitions; her appearance and outfit stay identical throughout; [light: warm sunset light / cool night lamps / soft morning light]; [style: anime style consistent with @Image1 / semi-realistic 2.5D illustration].

Safety tail (always last):

> Only she is in frame the whole time; no faces in frames, photos, posters or screens; no text, subtitles or watermarks anywhere. [If not English: She speaks only <language>, a little slowly.] Only voices and ambient sound effects, no background music.

Use "he" / "they" throughout when the character is not a woman.

## 7. Must-fix list (check before quoting)

- [ ] 2–4 quoted lines; English ≤ 28 words in total, first ≤ 8, last ≤ 14 (CJK ≤ 60 / 18 / 30 characters).
- [ ] The first sentence sets the scene only — no dialogue in it.
- [ ] "the first line starts before second 2" and "finishes this line by second 13 … two seconds of eye contact" are both there.
- [ ] Closing line: one continuous take, no cuts, appearance and outfit identical, light, style.
- [ ] Hands: both have something to do; no "grab / hold / pull the camera", no "selfie", nothing reaching toward the lens.
- [ ] No head turns: the payoff uses the eye-only glance ("she only glances toward it with her eyes; her head barely moves and her face stays toward the camera").
- [ ] No doors, doorways, corridors, glass doors, knocks, doorbells, footsteps or anyone approaching.
- [ ] One person only: no other hands, crowds, couples or passers-by.
- [ ] Directions say "the camera", never "you" or "the viewer" (the spoken lines may say "you").
- [ ] No names — character, franchise or brand. No microphones. No weapons or blood.
- [ ] No nudity, undressing, body parts, underwear, wet or see-through clothing; "fully dressed, the outfit stays in place" is enough.
- [ ] Adult characters in a grown-up setting only: no school, students, uniforms or family roles.
- [ ] The music rule is in the tail.
- [ ] Every attached image is named `@Image1` … `@ImageN`.

## 8. The 30-second version

- 8–10 beats of about 3 s: goal → obstacle → result, grown from the card's scenario and its hook; 3–6 lines, speech ≤ ~60% of the runtime; the last beat is a question the viewer can answer as their first chat message.
- A one-take instruction holds poorly at 30 s. Either write "one location, few cuts", or place two or three deliberate hard cuts at stated seconds.
- Keep the safety tail, the busy-hands and eye-only-glance rules, and end on a head-and-shoulders hold with no further push-in.

## 9. Worked example (15 s, original)

The hero image is attached as `@Image1` and shows her in the outfit described.

> A rooftop herb garden at dusk, strings of warm bulbs overhead, potted herbs on long wooden benches, the city skyline soft in the distance. She stands at a potting bench in a dark green knit sweater and straight jeans, sleeves pushed up, a watering can in one hand; her phone lies face-down beside a basil pot. @Image1 is her face, hair and outfit reference only; the scene is described here. She tips the watering can over the basil and, still pouring, looks up at the camera and speaks — the first line starts before second 2: "You found the roof." The camera stays at her eye level in a medium shot, nearly still; she sets the can down and leans on the bench, tucks a strand of hair behind her ear and says with a half-smile: "Careful. The basil bites." The phone buzzes against the wooden bench and its edge lights up; she only glances toward it with her eyes, her head barely moves and her face stays toward the camera; she tilts her head, then looks straight into the lens: "That's my boss. Let it ring. Stay until the lights go out?" She finishes this line by second 13, then her lips close, she raises an eyebrow and holds eye contact for two seconds. The whole clip keeps a playful, warm tension; one continuous long take with no cuts and no transitions; her appearance and outfit stay identical throughout; warm bulb light against blue dusk; anime style consistent with @Image1. Only she is in frame the whole time; no faces in frames, photos, posters or screens; no text, subtitles or watermarks anywhere. Only voices and ambient sound effects, no background music.

Lines: 4 + 4 + 12 = 20 words; hook 12 words. Staging: mid-task. Payoff: phone buzz. Ending: raised eyebrow.
